Understand the 124 series

A practical introduction to the model family, body-style codes and research habits that help avoid mixing specifications between variants.

Editorial note: this is an introductory overview, not a complete factory specification database. Exact equipment and engine combinations vary by market, production year and individual vehicle.

From 124 series to E-Class

Mercedes-Benz introduced the 124 model series in 1984. The range expanded across several body styles; in 1993, the saloon range adopted the E-Class name as Mercedes-Benz revised its model nomenclature. For an individual car, the build plate, VIN-derived records and period documentation are more reliable than badges alone.

How to identify a specific car

  • Record the model designation and production year from reliable documentation.
  • Confirm engine family and displacement from vehicle-specific records; do not infer from exterior badges alone.
  • Photograph trim, dashboard, engine bay and identification plates for private research, but redact sensitive identifiers before public sharing.
  • Check the applicable market and equipment specification before ordering parts or comparing performance figures.

Research sources

Start with Mercedes-Benz heritage information, period brochures or workshop documentation where available. Indian enthusiast archives add market context, but forum recollections should be labelled as owner experience unless independently verified.

What this guide deliberately avoids

It does not claim every global variant was sold in India, publish unverified engine combinations, or treat one owner's car as representative of all W124s. A future version should add model-specific records only after checking primary documentation.
